01 / CURRENT RESEARCH

Two research lines, one evolving practice

The portfolio is organised around questions rather than software or genres. LIVRAISON begins with invisible labour and the city. WHAT IS REAL? begins with synthetic identity and the social life of images.

01

FICTION FILM + RESEARCH · SCREENPLAY IN DEVELOPMENT

LIVRAISON

Invisible labour, urban movement and everyday technologies of delivery

IN DEVELOPMENT

LIVRAISON is a screenplay and research process, not a completed film. No fabricated production still is used to represent it.

Set in Oujda, the 20–22 minute fiction follows Kader, a 26-year-old university student working as a delivery rider. Algorithmic pressure, breakdowns, injury, medical costs and disputed orders gradually push him from individual improvisation toward documenting abuses and connecting with other riders. The ending offers no easy victory: a small committee begins to form.

TIME / ALGORITHMCountdowns, priority orders, waiting and platform pressure.
BODY / MACHINEFatigue, fall, breakdown, repair, medicine and the cost of continuing.
TRACE / EVIDENCEDoor photos, video, tickets, prescriptions, invoices and screenshots.
SOLIDARITYFriendship, messaging groups, mutual aid, meeting and collective organisation.

RESEARCH METHOD

The smartphone: order → proof → link

In the screenplay, the phone first directs Kader through countdowns and orders; then it becomes a tool for proving deliveries; later it helps riders collect incidents, breakdowns and claims into a shared archive.

OUJDA→KADER→COLLECTIVE

No production image is shown here because the film has not yet been shot. The visual field will be developed from observation, sound, routes, bodies, tools and traces.

02

VISUAL RESEARCH · DIGITAL TWIN · AUDIENCE BELIEF

WHAT IS REAL?

Synthetic presence, possible selves and the language that makes images socially real

2026 →

A recurring digital twin of myself becomes a laboratory for visual belief: the same synthetic identity can appear in places I never visited, professions I never held and events that never happened.

The research compares captured presence with generated presence and follows the language around the image: prompts, captions, biographies, comments and reposts can manufacture context, credibility and identity.

06 / PRACTICE

From technical camera practice to research-led cinema

My practice began with learning how to expose, light and frame an image. It is now increasingly concerned with what images do socially: what they reveal, hide, claim and make believable.

I work across fiction, cinematography, photography and documentary observation. Since 2018 I have maintained an independent professional audiovisual practice, while my 2023–2026 university studies in Cultural Industries and Cinematic Art helped me place that experience inside a broader reflection on cinema, culture and representation. I am now developing projects where research is not decoration around a film, but part of the cinematic method itself.
